BTS in Chile: Press Coverage Analysis

Sentiment Analysis · Applied Research · 2026
Python NLP Digital Trace

Overview

This notebook asks a concrete question: what does Chilean press coverage say about the dispute between BTS and Chilean authorities over the use of Estadio Nacional as a concert venue, and what tone predominates in that coverage? The source is a 483-article export collected between June 29 and July 6, 2026 with PressCL, filtered down from a raw scrape that mixed the Chilean story with unrelated noise — Madrid tour dates, Korean chart rankings, and foreign-language entertainment notes.

Context

The raw export bundled three distinct stories under the same band name: the Chilean venue dispute, general BTS/ARMY coverage unrelated to Chile, and syndicated noise from other markets. Isolating the venue conflict required a cleaning pipeline before any sentiment or topic analysis could be meaningful, since the volume and tone of the unrelated stories would otherwise dilute the signal.

Methods

Raw titles are first stripped of the outlet-name suffix appended during scraping, then deduplicated (955 → 762 rows) since the same headline is frequently republished across outlets. A language filter removes machine-translated and foreign-market entries (762 → 745), and a keyword-based relevance filter isolates coverage of the Chilean case specifically (745 → 483 final rows).

Sentiment is scored with pysentimiento's Spanish RoBERTuito model, run over the full article body when available and the headline otherwise. Because RoBERTuito truncates at 128 tokens, longer articles are split into ~100-token chunks, scored independently, and the resulting probabilities aggregated back into a single label per article. Each article is also assigned a topic category via keyword rules — the stadium authority's decision, the government's response, ARMY/fan reaction, alternative venue proposals, and a residual "Otros" bucket — which lets sentiment be cross-tabulated with topic and with time. A binomial GLM (statsmodels) then tests whether negative tone concentrates in specific categories or is spread evenly across the story.

Results

Across the 483 articles, coverage skews negative but not overwhelmingly so: 41.0% negative, 56.7% neutral, and 2.3% positive. The regression shows this split is not uniform across topics — coverage tagged as an alternative-venue proposal is significantly less likely to be negative (OR = 0.15, 95% CI [0.05, 0.52], p = .003) and significantly more likely to be neutral (OR = 2.61, 95% CI [1.18, 5.77], p = .018) than coverage of the stadium authority's decision itself. Coverage of the government's response, ARMY/fan reaction, and the residual "Otros" category does not differ significantly in tone from that reference story.
