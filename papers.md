  <summary><span class="paper-title">What spins the arrow? A multilevel analysis of LLM stances on Inglehart's postmaterialism index</span><span class="paper-meta">Bustamante, G., <strong>Barrientos, T.</strong> · PNAS Nexus · 2026</span><span class="paper-badge badge-review">Under review</span></summary>
  <div class="paper-abstract">Conversational AI systems increasingly support everyday decisions, and the value content of their recommendations varies across systems in ways users cannot observe. Studies of cultural bias and political lean report convergent content in language models, yet they treat each system as independent and leave unresolved whether that content is organized by the producer or by the release. This study applies the twelve-item Inglehart postmaterialism battery as a fixed comparative probe to 33 language models from 22 organizations, queried without system prompts or user context and with option order randomized on every call (17,786 choices). Using API and locally run models, responses were collected in Python and analyzed with multilevel logistic models in Stata 17. A three-level logistic model partitions variance in postmaterialist choice between organizations and models nested within them. Corporate provenance carries little systematic information. The organizational level accounts for 4.5% of latent variance, and only xAI separates from the overall mean. The model level adds 7.4 points, so releases differ more than producers. The remaining 88.1% lies within models, across repeated administrations of the same system. The model-level signal is asymmetric. Four hosted commercial releases hold a consistent materialist position, while the postmaterialist end consists largely of systems that balance one item of each kind or answer near chance. Item content shifts the outcome more than any producer except two. Elicited value content is therefore a probabilistic tendency concentrated in specific releases, recoverable only as an average over many administrations.</div>
</details>

  <summary><span class="paper-title">Life course trajectories of civic engagement: evidence from Chile</span><span class="paper-meta"><strong>Barrientos, T.</strong>, Biehl, A. · <em>Voluntas</em> · 2026</span><span class="paper-badge badge-review">Under review</span></summary>
  <div class="paper-abstract">Civic engagement research largely focuses on high-trust, high-participation societies, leaving open how life-course processes shape participation in low-trust, family-centered contexts. This article reconstructs civic engagement trajectories among older Chileans (born 1938 to 1963) using sequence and cluster analysis of retrospective life-history calendar data, followed by a multinomial logistic regression of trajectory membership. Four trajectories emerge, ranging from prolonged non-engagement, the modal pattern, to sustained, intense involvement, indicating that low aggregate engagement conceals a stable minority who maintain or increase their participation across the life course. Years of cohabitation with a partner predict a higher probability of engaged trajectories, contradicting the region's dominant thesis that familism constrains civic life, while years of cohabitation with children show no association. Gender differentiates trajectories primarily through persistence, with men overrepresented in the two sustained-engagement trajectories and women concentrated in prolonged non-engagement. Family life can therefore facilitate civic engagement in low-trust settings.</div>
</details>

  <summary><span class="paper-title">Informal Territory and Housing Trajectory of LGBT+ People: The Laurelia Arcoíris informal settlement, Tomé, Chile</span><span class="paper-meta">Méndez, N., <strong>Barrientos, T.</strong>, Vergara, C. · <em>INVI</em> · 2025</span><span class="paper-badge badge-review">Under review</span></summary>
  <div class="paper-abstract"><p>The right to housing, declared universal by the International Covenant on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights, is still not reflected equally for LGBT+ people in Chile, where subsidised housing policies tend to exclude this group. This study addresses the situation of housing exclusion faced by the LGBT+ population in Chile, focusing on the Laurelia Arcoíris informal settlement in Tomé, Biobío Region, where 60% of the households identify as LGBT+. The methodology employed includes the collection of statistical data and interviews with residents to explore their life trajectories, their reasons for living in a camp and how they have constructed their residential habitat. The results show that exclusion from formal housing policies forces many LGBT+ people to seek alternatives such as encampments. However, in Laurelia Arcoíris, residents have managed to develop a strong sense of belonging and security, finding an environment that allows them to overcome the marginalisation and stigma they face in other contexts, which underlines the importance of considering diversity in the formulation of public housing policies.</p><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> Informal settlement, residential habitat, informality, LGBT+ population.</p><p><strong>Resumen.</strong> El derecho a la vivienda, declarado universal por el Pacto Internacional de Derechos Económicos, Sociales y Culturales, aún no se refleja equitativamente para las personas LGBT+ en Chile, donde las políticas habitacionales subsidiarias tienden a excluir a este grupo. Este estudio aborda la situación de exclusión habitacional que enfrenta la población LGBT+ en Chile, focalizándose en el campamento Laurelia Arcoíris, un asentamiento informal en Tomé, Región del Biobío, donde el 60% de los hogares se identifican como LGBT+. La metodología empleada incluye la recolección de datos estadísticos y entrevistas a los residentes para explorar sus trayectorias de vida, sus razones para habitar en un campamento y cómo han construido su hábitat residencial. Los resultados muestran que la exclusión de las políticas habitacionales formales obliga a muchas personas LGBT+ a buscar alternativas como los campamentos. Sin embargo, en Laurelia Arcoíris, los residentes han logrado desarrollar un fuerte sentido de pertenencia y seguridad, encontrando un entorno que les permite superar la marginación y el estigma que enfrentan en otros contextos, lo que subraya la importancia de considerar la diversidad en la formulación de políticas públicas habitacionales.</p><p><strong>Palabras clave:</strong> Campamento, hábitat residencial, informalidad, población LGBT+.</p></div>

<details class="paper">
  <summary><span class="paper-title">Who sings the change? Artist turnover and thematic change in Chilean popular lyrics, 2000-2025</span><span class="paper-meta"><strong>Barrientos, T.</strong>, Bustamante, G. · Prepared for the <em>Journal of Cultural Analytics</em></span></summary>
  <div class="paper-abstract"><p>Research on thematic change in popular lyrics speaks of trends, and the term covers two different quantities. A trend can describe how the repertoire of a musical field changes as artists enter and leave it, and it can describe how the artists who remain in the field change what they write. Song-weighted designs, which treat each song as an observation, report a mixture of the two. This article separates them for Chilean popular music between 2000 and 2025. The study fits a structural topic model with eight topics to 8,572 Spanish-language songs by 363 artists, retrieved from Genius and benchmarked against musicapopular.cl, an encyclopaedic compilation of 2,599 Chilean artists, and it estimates change between 2000-2004 and 2020-2025 with songs and with artists as the unit, with cohorts reweighted to the benchmark and with an artist-block bootstrap. Four topics change under every estimand, with Perreo and Desire and Gangster Lifestyle rising and Heartbreak and Nostalgia and Protest and Social Critique declining. Counting artists halves both rises, and three declines that are significant when songs are counted lose their interval. A two-period decomposition attributes between 53 and 76 percent of each change to the turnover of debut cohorts, which is a lower bound on artist turnover. Re-estimation recovers only the Perreo and Desire topic consistently, so the article reports the remaining topics as features of one exploratory representation. The article concludes that thematic change in the indexed Chilean field is predominantly a change in who is recorded, and that song-weighted designs report this change as if it were a change in what is sung.</p><p><strong>Keywords:</strong> song lyrics, structural topic model, Chile, artist turnover, cultural change, compositional decomposition</p></div>
</details>
